# Radiation Sources: Multi-classifier Fusion for Open Set Specific Radiation Source Identification [with Python code]

Abstract: To ensure the privacy and security of Internet of Things (IoT) systems, specific radiation source identification technology utilizes hardware characteristics to identify device identities. In response to the growing demand for identifying unknown devices, this paper focuses on the problem of open set specific radiation source identification. Firstly, by exploring the working mechanism of radio signals and the principles of open set identification, a problem model for open set specific radiation source identification is constructed. The study indicates that feature overlap is a challenging issue in open set specific radiation source identification, due to the fingerprint feature extractor being unable to cluster features of unknown devices or distinguish them from known device features. Considering that feature overlap can lead to incorrect identification of unknown devices, this paper proposes the integration of multiple classifiers at the decision-making layer to improve recognition accuracy and recall rate. The study employs three different input methods and four fusion methods to achieve multi-classifier integration. Experiments based on data collected from Huanghua Airport demonstrate that the proposed method can avoid feature space overlap issues, achieving higher accuracy and recall rates.
Introduction: With the popularization of IoT technology and related products, privacy and security issues have become increasingly prominent. To prevent network attacks and malicious deception, IoT systems must verify device identities [1,2]. Key authentication, as the most common form of identity verification, faces the risk of password cracking. This risk has driven the introduction of specific radiation source identification (SEI) technology into the field of IoT security and privacy protection. Unlike key authentication, SEI uses external fingerprint features and comparative analysis of hardware characteristics to identify device identities [3]. As it leverages the uniqueness of hardware for identification, SEI has advantages in detecting spoofing attacks and relay attacks. Besides being used independently, it can also be combined with key authentication to form a dual authentication system, further ensuring privacy and security. Additionally, as a new signal processing method in remote sensing, SEI also shows significant advantages in ship monitoring, air traffic control, and target tracking.
Currently, most research focuses on how to extract fingerprint features as classification markers, such as entropy [4], statistical measures [5], and fractals [6]. By drawing from methods in the fields of image processing, speech recognition, and defect detection [7], signals are input into convolutional neural networks (CNN) [4,8-13] and long short-term memory networks (LSTM) [14]. These neural networks have diverse inputs, including time-frequency spectra [4,9,11], bispectra [8,10], and recursive graphs [11]. Notably, raw coherent and orthogonal sampling points can directly be input into a one-dimensional convolutional neural network [9,12,13]. In addition to traditional network architectures, novel neural networks also play a role in certain scenarios, such as adversarial networks (AN) for adapting to frequency changes [15] and generative adversarial networks (GAN) for mitigating deliberate modulation effects [16,17]. These methods construct specific feature spaces through corresponding feature extractors to achieve device identity recognition. Numerous researchers have attempted to apply feature fusion to specific radiation source identification to improve recognition confidence [5,18-20], with results indicating that feature fusion significantly enhances recognition accuracy. The aforementioned methods achieve excellent recognition accuracies under the assumption of closed sets, but as IoT devices become more widely used, existing specific radiation source recognition systems inevitably face the challenge of identifying unknown devices. Therefore, addressing the problem of open set specific radiation source recognition (i.e., how to identify known devices while excluding unknown ones) is crucial.
The Open Set Recognition (OSR) is used to handle unknown scenarios that have not been learned during the model training process [21]. It has been widely applied in multiple fields such as face recognition, speech recognition, and object detection. Scholars have attempted to apply mature OSR techniques to specific radiation source identification, including one-to-one classification, OpenMax, and twin networks [22,23,24]. These methods can achieve specific radiation source identification under the assumption of open set, but specific radiation source identification faces unique challenges. The goal of specific radiation source identification is to identify hardware differences caused by process limitations [25], which are unpredictable due to the unpredictability of hardware defects. Therefore, feature extractors struggle to cluster the features of unknown devices, leading to random distribution or falling into known clusters in the feature space. Additionally, the lack of prior information about unknown devices may cause overlap between unknown clusters and known clusters, making it difficult to distinguish between unknown and known clusters. Both situations result in the overlap of features between unknown and known devices, making it challenging for unknown devices to be effectively rejected.
To address this issue, this paper proposes a method for specific radiation source identification based on multi-classifier fusion. Multi-classifier fusion reduces the recognition error of overlapping features by combining the identity declarations of multiple feature extractors for joint decision-making. The method includes three independent specific radiation source identification channels and a classifier combination. The input to each channel varies, including co-phase and orthogonal sampling points, spectrum, and amplitude and phase. Each channel uses a 18-layer residual network (1d) as a fingerprint feature extractor. During training, each channel uses softmax as a classifier for training; during testing, each channel uses softmax thresholding or OpenMax as a classifier for identity declaration, then fuses the identity declarations through the classifier combination to enhance recognition performance.

Conclusion: To address the issue of open-set identification in the field of Internet of Things security, this paper establishes a mathematical model and proves that the unique challenge of identifying specific radiation sources is feature fusion. In response to this problem, a method for identifying specific radiation sources based on multi-classifier fusion is proposed. This method constructs three fingerprint feature extractors through residual networks for three inputs: coherent orthogonal sampling points, spectrum, and amplitude phase. The identity declaration of these three feature extractors is fused by a classifier combiner to overcome the drawback of feature fusion. Experiments conducted at Huanghua Airport demonstrate that the proposed method can improve the accuracy of the recognition system and the recall rate for unknown unknown class (UUC) devices.
